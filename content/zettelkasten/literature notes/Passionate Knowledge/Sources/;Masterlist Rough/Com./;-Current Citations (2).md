“The sensible qualities give both the power of working and the capacity of existence”

White's conclusion: the ratio or notion [the Latin has only Ratio] in a thing is That, by which its nature is fitted to the Understanding, or the Understandablenesse of a thing, or the quiddity, the whatnesse and, consequently, that, whereby a thing is made to Be: The Form, therefore, primarily, is the Cause [Ratio], or the whereby the Being is had. 




___

essentia and esse are one. - Same as in Spinoza. 

The very essence of things is their intelligibilitas; the way they are “fitted to
understanding.” It is not simply that nature can be understood – the way it is
given to understanding is what it is. The ratio of a thing is not only what it is
known by – it is “whereby a thing is made to Be.” Moreover: this essence –
the forma – is also the reason (ratio again) for its existence; essentia and esse are
one: “The sensible qualities give [a natural Ens of Thing] both the power of
working and the capacity of existence.”

This is an idea White could have found in neither the ancient nor the modern ontologies he was exploring and intwining - When reading through Wolfson, remember.
