Goclenius on Intuition: 

This resemblance between Descartes' intuition and that of the Scholastics has also been noted in Ariew (2011).

My comment appears before [cit.66]. Ariew compares Descartes' view of intuition with that of the Scholastics (as summarised by Goclenius).  

Goclenius goes on to identify “conception” as synonymous with concept; the latter, he specifies, is either clear or obscure—another celebrated Cartesian distinction—but here that distinction is given a theological reference: “The Scholastics employ this distinction when they deal with the conception of God. For they call the abstractive obscure; that, moreover, is what is made through the species of created things or as it shines in created things: the intuitive indeed, which is as it is in itself, they say is clear.” Though applied to the conception of God, not to human conception, this account recalls the “intuition” of Descartes’ early Rules, the incomplete forerunner of the Meditations. Clear and distinct ideas are the successors to intuitions as the units of Cartesian knowledge. (p. 114)

For the lack of intuition outside of the Regulae, and how Descartes' changes Suarez's conception of intuition, see; de Buzon & Kambouchner (2002)

de Buzon and Kambouchner (2002), in their Le vocabulaire de Descartes, also point out that Suarez's conception of intuition ("l’intuition intellectuelle"), only belongs to "pure intelligences" and will only be available in the "next life". They then point out the apparent inconsistency where:

"In Rule III, on the contrary, not only does this intellectual intuition appear possible, but it constitutes the fundamental act of knowledge, outside of which no science is possible." 

They also back up your other claim, that "the Latin term 'intuitus', which designates the fullness of visual or intellectual perception, is barely encountered outside the Rules."

"Le mot français d’intuition n’appartient pas au lexique de Descartes. Et à la différence du verbe intueri, le terme latin d’intuitus, qui désigne la plénitude de la perception visuelle ou intellectuelle, se rencontre peu en dehors des Regulae. Dans la tradition scolastique, la connaissance intuitive a été distinguée de la connaissance discursive (qui s’obtient par raisonnement), puis de la connaissance abstractive (qui est seulement médiate et toujours partielle). Selon de nombreux auteurs, dont Suarez, seule la perception sensible représente dans cette vie le mode intuitif de la connaissance : l’intuition intellectuelle qui appartient aux intelligences pures ne nous sera accessible que dans l’autre vie. Dans la Règle III, au contraire, non seulement cette intuition intellectuelle apparaît possible, mais elle constitue l’acte fondamental de la connaissance, en dehors duquel il n’y a pas de science possible." (p. 62)

de Buzon, Frédéric & Kambouchner, Denis (2002). Le vocabulaire de Descartes. Ellipses.
