1. Options for treatment or management of a condition, including the option of not providing treatment. 
	1. Consequences of providing the treatment vs consequences of not providing the treatment 
2. Aim of planned procedure or treatment. 
	1. Including side effects or other consequences. 
3. Details of the planned procedure or treatment, it's benefits chances of success
	1. Details of the management of side effects. 
4. Details of secondary interventions that may be required and patients consent for those interventions 
	1. E.g. Blood transfusion 
5. Who will be performing the procedure and what doctors in training will be involved. 
6. A reminder that they can change their mind at any point and can seek a second opinion 
7. Any costs that the patient may incur. 

Finally, enough time should be given for the patient to properly weight up the pros and cons of this. 

Determining Competence of a Patient 

If an adult is deemed not competent, doctors should abide by either:
1. A living will, directives left by the patient at a previous time
2. Their best judgement informed by the judgement and wishes of the relatives. 

Gillick competence or Fraser Guidelines:
1. A five year old child can give consent for a small cut but not a major surgical operation. 
2. Although a child can give consent for a treatment if competent, they cannot refuse treatment that is deemed to be in their best interest. 
	1. The decision needs to be made by his parents 
3. If both parents refuse to give consent for a treatment which is life saving. Doctors would need to act in the best interest of the child, sometimes this includes getting a court order, or performing the treatment first and then getting the court order. 

When can you breach confidentiality? 
1. Implied consent, such a family member coming along for a consultation 
2. Court Order
3. Public interest and protect the patients 
	1. Notification to the authorities of a notifiable disease (mumps, measles etc)
4. Suspected cases of child abuse and neglect. 
5. DVLA if the patients condition affects his driving 
6. Information can help in a court case in limited scenarios
