Bystandar Intervention Questions:
Examples:

- **Direct** — address it yourself, calmly and directly.
- **Distract** — redirect the conversation/topic to defuse tension without confrontation.
- **Delegate** — involve someone with more authority or appropriate standing (here: the tutor, explicitly).
- **Delay** — check in with the affected person afterward if you can't act in the moment.
- **Document** — note what happened if it needs to be escalated later.

What are the Issues Questions:
Levels-of-analysis scan (the most versatile one)

Work outward in concentric circles so you don't just fixate on the most obvious issue:

- **Individual level** — what's going on for each person involved (motivations, emotions, wellbeing).
- **Interpersonal level** — the relationship/dynamic between people (conflict, power imbalance, communication breakdown).
- **Systemic/institutional level** — policies, hierarchy, resourcing, culture that shaped the situation.
- **Societal/ethical level** — broader principles or precedents at stake (fairness, rights, professional norms).

___

Priorities Questions:
Heirarchies: 
- Safety/life > time-sensitive harm > reversible inconvenience
- Immediate/acute > urgent > important-but-not-urgent
- Duty to a vulnerable party > duty to bystanders > duty to self

SAFER Acronym, Survery (what do I know), Assess (put on heirarchy), Focus (on highest priority), Engage (appropriate help), Return (to the lower priority issue)

Ethical Questions
- **D**efine the problem — what is the actual ethical dilemma/tension?
- **E**xplore the alternatives — what are the possible courses of action?
- **C**onsider the consequences — for each alternative, who is affected and how?
- **I**dentify your values/principles — which ethical principles apply (autonomy, beneficence, etc.)?
- **D**ecide and act — commit to a course of action based on the above.
- **E**valuate the outcome — reflect on whether the decision achieved its goal, and what you'd learn for next time.
