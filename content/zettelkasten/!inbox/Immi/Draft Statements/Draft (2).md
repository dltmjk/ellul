The cancellation had an adverse effect on each member of the family. Our eldest son, Ahan, who was keen on continuing his education in Australia and fulfilling his dream of becoming a doctor, became quite withdrawn and quiet in the days following the "Section 128 - Verification of Contact Details" email. He started to cancel scheduled social events with his friends and lost his appetite; both behaviours were very unusual for him. I also noticed that he was awake more hours of the night and slept more during the day. Two of his major hobbies, reading and learning languages, had stopped entirely. When we heard that he had paused his classes with his language teachers for no specific reason, my wife and I became exceptionally worried, as this was one of the highlights of his week.

Apart from Ahan, the entire family felt blindsided by the sudden cancellation decision. We had all planned to visit Ahan in Australia for his rescheduled graduation in October. However, the cancellation and the possible PIC 4013 ban affected these plans. In particular, we were concerned about how our son would be able to cope with the sudden change in his life plans and what path he would take if returning to Australia became impossible. We knew that he was accepted into multiple master's programs at universities in the United Kingdom. However, as parents, we did not want to send him overseas in such a dejected state, especially to a new country. Yet, given his lack of other alternatives, in the case of being unable to apply for an Australian visa to pursue a medical degree, which is his first choice, he became quite depressed. This caused significant stress for my wife and me. We needed to say something, but we were unsure of what exactly to say to him in this state.

We had attempted to maintain some semblance of solidarity in the face of the situation for both our children. In private, however, we were uncertain of how to properly respond to such a situation. In particular, it was the complete lack of information that led to this distinct feeling of helplessness. While we eventually received a Section 129 notice, it was that long month between the "Verification of Contact Details" and the notice itself in which our family suffered the most. We were unable to draw any conclusion about the possible ramifications a Section 128 cancellation would have, and we became convinced that it would result in a three-year ban. The possibility of medical school aside, our son has all of his belongings in Australia, items that he had cherished for a decade and had taken with him. While removalists were an option, we were hesitant, given worries about the care that these items would receive.

The Section 129 notice did not make the situation better. Now we had to involve lawyers and draft multiple documents as part of our response. My job has already been incredibly stressful over the past few months, and this extra issue came at nearly the worst time. Ahan, who was already revising for some early medical school interviews, now had to pause his preparation to deal with the ongoing situation. The entire process proved to be incredibly stressful for all members of the family and caused a large amount of unnecessary psychological distress.
