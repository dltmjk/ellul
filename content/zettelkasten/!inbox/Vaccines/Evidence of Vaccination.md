Hepatitis B: you need both a vaccine course and a blood test
- **Course:** an age-appropriate course.
    - Aged 20 or over: 3 adult doses at 0, 1, 4 months or 0, 2, 4 months. Longer gaps are fine.
    - If vaccinated younger: 3 paediatric doses, or 2 adult doses at 0 and 6 months if given at ages 11–15.
- **Serology:** anti-HBs of at least 10 mIU/mL, taken at least 4 weeks after your last dose.
    - Alternatively, evidence of past infection (anti-HBc and/or HBsAg+) is accepted.
- If your serology is below 10, you’ll need a booster and then repeat serology at least 4 weeks later. Temporary compliance is allowed for this.
- If you haven’t finished a course, you’ll need to complete one. Temporary compliance is allowed for this too.
- **Critical:** if you have a completed course on record, you must submit serology with your first upload. Otherwise your documents won’t be processed, and temporary compliance won’t be given.

Varicella (chickenpox)

- **Vaccine:** 1 dose if vaccinated under age 14, or 2 doses at least 4 weeks apart if vaccinated at 14 or older.
- **Or serology:** a positive IgG result.
- A verbal history of having had chickenpox isn’t accepted.

___

**MMR (measles, mumps, rubella)**

- **Vaccine:** 2 doses at least one month apart.
- **Or serology:** positive IgG for all three.
    - If any result is negative, equivocal or low, you need 2 MMR doses, with at least one given after the serology date. Repeat serology isn’t needed.
- **Or:** born before 1966.

Tuberculosis (only if the TB Assessment Tool flags you)

- This applies if you were born in, or spent 3 or more months in total in, a high-TB-incidence country.
- You’ll need an IGRA blood test (preferred) or a TST skin test.
- If the result is positive, you must be reviewed and cleared by an NSW Health Chest Clinic, in Australia.
- If you had a TST that was negative and you’ve had BCG before, you need a second TST 1–5 weeks after the first.

Blood Borne Virus screening (Medicine and Dentistry)

- You need screening every 3 years.
- You only submit the BBV Declaration, with your test date and each box initialled. Don’t send the test results.

**NSW Health Vaccination Record Card** (if your history is incomplete or you have no AIR; most international students use this)

- Take the blank card from your zip file to a doctor. The University Health Service is recommended.
- Put your full name and date of birth at the top of both pages.
- Every entry must be dated, signed and stamped by a medical practitioner. This includes:
    - historical vaccines
    - all serology results
    - TB results
- The doctor doesn’t need to be the one who originally vaccinated you.
- The rubella serology lab report is the only lab report to attach. All other serology goes on the card only.
- Include the dTpa batch number. If you don’t have it, you must also upload your AIR to verify the dTpa dose.
