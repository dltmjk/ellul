She was losing her hearing. 
It started small, more shouting in the kitchen with less certainty in her face. He'd find himself repeating every call, not that he minded, but instead wondered how she managed to convince herself nothing was wrong. The fault always laid with the other person

"You weren't yelling loud enough"

They say when a child loses his hearing he can still make out phantasmal voices coming from the lips of his loved ones. 

This is to say that he knew long before she did. In the same situation, he felt like the truth is always so immediate. That any action at all was a performance, and he was never sure when performances stopped. He couldn't say because nobody had ever told him. 



I knew long before she did. It started small, she wouldn't hear me in the other room. I'd have to repeat myself more often.

He remembered when he was young, how once he took out his homework from his book bag and placed it all neatly on the table, took out his pens and arranged them beside his work, all while his mother hadn't woken yet and when she eventually did rise and find him studiously scribbling. All he could remember was that she was unexpressably happy, and in that memory was the feeling of daylight breaking through the window. That was everything for him. Heaven itself seemed to be a sort of memory. 

He took out the manuscripts from his bag finished with his scratchy handwriting from at least five different pens. His apartment was dim with light. The desk was the focal point of the room and whatever little light made itself known through the window landed directly on a stack of books piled high. It was covered with paper, geological in formation, those touching wood must've been at least a year old. Probably dating from when he first moved in. The top layer was constantly being remade, books were being swiped away and stacked. Those on the bottom layer had become pedestrian, a relic of another person with antiquated taste. At a certain point they gained the permanence of furniture. 

At this time of day he felt it was necessary to eat. Not knowing exactly what, he decided to sit down on the couch, the only other piece of furniture in his apartment. It was an old brown leather couch which he found on the street shortly after moving in. He does not own a bed.
