He had been waiting for at least an hour now. They had promised "slightly past two", and now it was nearly 3. He briefly thought about calling them but he didn't know what he would say, and hoped that the context of his ringing would speak for itself.

On the 18th of December, in a room or maybe an office. Maybe a country east of somewhere, perhaps landlocked. An adolescent boy, now aged thirty, at the time aged 18 standing completely still, thought about his life as he would proceed to live it, then the later man of 30 sitting with his hands slightly fidgeting thinking about that boy of eighteen who he once was but now has completely escaped him. He is surrounded by a landscape or maybe by concrete, and he has not looked outside the window for the past few days. Both of them share the same fears inherited from the child of only five years of age collecting neuroticisms like he collected snails and slugs.

Even now, he hadn't realised exactly what had happened. He knew that it had happened, but he knew it like a fact, the same way he knew that the Earth was in the Milky Way, or that the yellow sun in the sky was actually a white ball of fire. He knew that despite never having seen a white flame in his life. He could say all this because other people had told him, the same way they'd been telling him about John. 

He must've known a little, otherwise he wouldn't have been running around like he did, calling people, scheduling cars and flights. What he still didn't know, prevented the one thing he needed to be doing. 

He was like a child hearing his parents argue, understanding their anger but not what their words mean. Perhaps this explained why was able to handle it with such ease. Dealing tactfully when people would walk up to him to apologize, for his tremendous loss. The tact was in not correcting them. If there was a loss he needed to be shown it. 

Maybe it was because he knew that nothing was as terrifying as he originally imagined it to be. No failure had stung or suffocated him like he assumed they would. Humiliation never came exactly like how he had thought it would, it never took the shape he supposed it to be. After all, everything still had to be lived through, so death (being nothing new to life) would likely be the same. In fact, it would proably feel like slipping into a familiar bed with familiar sheets after a long day. Freshly washed and dried clothes that you haven't worn since you were a child. 

Or maybe it was because he could still imagine John, in-between this pointless exercise of mourning him,  waiting at home. Wondering where everybody was. 


He was reminded of that time he was alone in his parents house, clearing their belongings after they both passed. It was warm, or at least it felt warm, or maybe it was because he strongly remembered the sun, and the sunlight as instantly filled the now empty room. Death also seemed similar to this. Though he couldn't tell you exactly why. John was full of these associations, and it was recently that he started to realize the sheer number. He was like a unifying principle so large and simple that absolutely everything could be traced back to him, his fingerprints were rubbed against everything. He could hear his words coming from nowhere.
